## Check the Sentence, Not the Count

Scite's [features](https://scite.ai/features) describe Smart Citations, which
show citation passages and classify their relationship to the cited work.
The provider's [literature review guide](https://scite.ai/blog/how-to-use-ai-for-literature-review)
distinguishes those signals from a researcher's own assessment.

Try one public paper you already know. Open a later citation and compare its
surrounding paragraph with the exact claim you want to make. A citation may
agree about a method while saying nothing about the result you intend to cite.
Read for the relevant setting, sample, or time period before reusing it.
If you cannot see the needed passage, mark the claim as unverified.

## Recheck the Evidence Before Reusing It

Keep the claim, the original paper, the citing passage, and your reason for
using or excluding it. When you revisit the claim, check for a newer version
or a notice at the publisher, and read later work for its own context.
Do not interpret a label or the absence of a citation as a verdict.

Check the [pricing page](https://scite.ai/pricing),
[privacy policy](https://scite.ai/policy), and any institution rules before
relying on account features or submitting unpublished material. Use the
[citation practice pack](/use-cases/check-whether-a-citation-supports-a-claim/)
to rehearse the distinction between a relevant-looking reference and one
that actually supports the sentence.
