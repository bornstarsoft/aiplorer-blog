## Test the SVG, Not Just the Preview

Recraft's [vector generation page](https://www.recraft.ai/ai-vector-generator)
describes producing vector graphics from a prompt. Use a fictional project
to test one simple sign or icon with flat shapes. Open the exported SVG
in the recipient's editor. Change one fill and resize the design. Check
that the intended paths remain selectable, the edges stay readable at the
smallest intended size, and nothing is clipped.

## Leave an Asset a Teammate Can Revise

The [vector editor guide](https://www.recraft.ai/blog/vector-editor) shows
changes that can be made in Studio. Keep the candidate SVG, the approved
reference, and notes about edits you verified in the destination editor.
Repeat those checks when an icon joins a larger set or the palette changes.
The [export plan practice pack](/use-cases/check-an-ai-export-plan-against-delivery-requirements/)
offers a way to rehearse checking a delivered file against its brief.

Check current [pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/pricing),
[terms](https://www.recraft.ai/legal/terms), and
[privacy policy](https://www.recraft.ai/legal/privacy). Recraft's terms
describe possible public visibility for some assets, including free-tier
output. Do not put client marks or unreleased designs into a trial.
For client or public work, separately verify the rights to all references
and the intended use of the final asset.
