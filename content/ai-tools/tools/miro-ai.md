## Check One Group Against the Original Notes

Miro's [sticky-note guide](https://help.miro.com/hc/en-us/articles/28781881506834-Miro-AI-with-Sticky-notes)
describes using AI to group selected notes. Try it with fictional notes
from an equipment request workshop. Include two notes that say "approval
delay" for different reasons: one requester did not know who could
approve, while another was waiting for a stock check. Include one note
about an accessibility need. Does the proposed grouping keep those
differences visible?

## Compare Notes at the Next Workshop

Keep the original notes and record which notes were included, which group
labels you changed, and which questions remain open. When more notes
arrive, check those questions again without treating a larger group as
proof of agreement. The [meeting actions practice pack](/use-cases/check-ai-meeting-actions-against-decisions/)
offers another way to distinguish discussion from agreed decisions.

Before using real notes, review [plans](https://miro.com/pricing/),
[AI credits](https://help.miro.com/hc/en-us/articles/19756209116178-Miro-AI-credits),
[privacy](https://miro.com/legal/privacy-policy/), board access, and
[AI data settings](https://help.miro.com/hc/en-us/articles/22874000865554-Miro-AI-quality-improvements).
