## Start With the Claim That Matters

Pick the sentence that would change what you do. Copy it into the claim log before rewriting it. Check that sentence against the original source, not just the AI's summary or a search snippet.

### 1. Check What the Source Actually Measured

Find the passage behind the claim. Match the group, timeframe, and measure. If you cannot open the source or find the passage, mark the claim **Unverified**. A missing source does not prove the claim false.

**Try it:** [Check whether a citation supports a claim](/use-cases/check-whether-a-citation-supports-a-claim/).

### 2. Read Quotes With the Lines Around Them

Check the speaker, wording, and conditions. A quote can be verbatim but still misleading if the brief leaves out what the speaker was responding to. If you rewrite it, make clear it is a paraphrase.

**Try it:** [Check an AI quote against a transcript](/use-cases/check-an-ai-quote-against-a-transcript/).

### 3. Put the Missing Limits Back

Compare the conclusion with the underlying sources. Preserve disagreements, corrections, and decisions still pending. Do not turn an observation into a causal claim or combine numbers that refer to different measures.

**Try it:** [Retest AI summaries with three reference checks](/use-cases/retest-ai-summaries-with-a-reference-pack/).

## Example: Completion Is Not Speed

An AI draft says a new onboarding guide cut training time. In this fictional example, the trial log records eight completions in one team. It contains no elapsed times or comparison group. A participant said it felt faster **after a mentor clarified the first step**.

The brief can report the eight completions and describe the participant's impression with its condition. It should not claim a measured speed improvement. If speed would change the decision, record how you would measure it in a follow-up.

## Make the Next Check Easy

Record a page number, section, or timestamp and the specific evidence gap. Keep the original claim next to any narrower wording. On a later visit, reopen unresolved sources first. Recheck claims whose source or wording has changed; do not silently overwrite the earlier review.
