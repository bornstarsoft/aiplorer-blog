## Start With The Tools You Actually Use

Choose a small set of announcements from [Change Watch](/ai-tools/review-updates/#tool-changes),
using your saved tools or a specific tool and topic. Download a brief of the
visible results for a discussion or a later check. It includes public summaries,
dates and source links, not private shortlist notes.

The selected tools can still reveal your interests or workflow. Inspect the
file before sharing it, and keep any internal decisions you add in an approved
location. Downloading is not an approval, a completed test or a read check.

## Keep Announcements Separate From Evidence

This is a fictional exercise, not a vendor announcement or measured test.
Imagine two notices: one changes a slide export workflow; another changes an
assistant's requested repository permissions. Your team uses both workflows,
but has not yet checked whether either notice applies to its accounts.

An unhelpful handoff would be: "Both tools changed. Upgrade and reconnect."
That invents a decision without checking scope, dependencies or permissions.

Instead, prepare a short review:

### Slide Export Workflow

- **Question:** Does this apply to the account and delivery format we use?
- **Next step:** Check the official scope, then export one approved sample if needed.
- **Evidence:** Open the saved output in the intended viewer, or record why the change does not apply.

### Repository Permissions

- **Question:** Does the requested access match this approved task?
- **Next step:** Ask the responsible owner to compare the current and requested scope.
- **Evidence:** Record the owner's scope review. A successful generated answer is not a permission review.

Do not upload customer slides, private repository contents or secrets simply
to complete the exercise. Do not grant broader access to make a test pass.

## End With A Decision, Not A Vague Reminder

For each notice, record one of these outcomes:

- **Not applicable to this workflow:** record the scope difference and what would trigger another check.
- **Test needed:** name a small non-sensitive task, expected result and owner.
- **Owner review needed:** record the unresolved permission, dependency or account question.
- **Checked for this sample:** record the observed result and remaining limits; do not call it universally safe.

A filled example of the second outcome might read:

```text
Notice: fictional slide export change
Applicable scope: not yet confirmed for our account
Next step: owner checks scope; if applicable, try the approved two-slide sample
Expected check: text, images and layout survive in the intended viewer
Owner: to be assigned during the discussion
Result: NOT RUN
Next review: after scope is confirmed and the sample is available
```

An export date tells you when the brief was created, not when its sources were
last reviewed. The brief retains the recorded announcement and source-check
dates. Read the linked official source again when a decision depends on it.

## Leave A Clear Starting Point For Next Time

Keep the evidence and decisions with the team record. Aiplorer's browser-local
shortlist can hold a brief non-sensitive reminder, but it is not a shared
workspace or evidence archive. Reading state is also separate from the decision:
leave an unresolved notice unread if that helps you find it again.

On your next visit, check the open question before collecting more notices.
The brief is a snapshot and will not update itself. Newly recorded or revised
announcements may need a fresh review; absence from Aiplorer is not proof that
a service is unchanged.
