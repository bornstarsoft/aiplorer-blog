## Three Checks Before You Send

Imagine you are preparing a PDF handout for a fictional open studio. AI drafted it from a public event brief and a separate staff planning note. Before sharing the file, check what it says, what it reveals, and what the recipient can actually open.

Do the checks in order. Fixing the document after export means you need to check the replacement file again.

### 1. Verify the Public Facts

Compare the draft with the organizer's approved brief. Check the venue, booking status, and promises about the event. If the brief leaves something open, leave it open in the handout too.

**Try it:** [Check an AI event announcement against a brief](/use-cases/check-an-ai-event-announcement-against-a-brief/).

### 2. Review What Will Leave Your Team

Remove staff-only notes and identifiers the audience does not need. Check titles, footers, captions, and link destinations as well as the body. Review comments and document properties where applicable; a cleaner-looking page is not enough.

**Try it:** [Check an AI redaction before sharing](/use-cases/check-an-ai-redaction-before-sharing/).

### 3. Open the Actual Export

Test the exact file you will attach or upload. Check the required page size, select and search for text, and verify that links reach the approved destinations with the intended level of access. The written export plan does not count as an export test.

**Try it:** [Check an AI export plan against delivery requirements](/use-cases/check-an-ai-export-plan-against-delivery-requirements/).

## Record What Would Stop You

In the open studio example, the handout matches the approved brief and no longer includes the staff note. But the schedule link in the final PDF points to the staff copy. **Hold the release.** Replace the link with the approved public destination, re-export, and test that replacement file before sharing it.

Use the checklist once per version. Record what you checked and what remains open. When you return, start with the unresolved item. If the brief, audience, or export changes, rerun the checks that depend on it. Keep private evidence in an approved location, not in a copy of the checklist you share.
