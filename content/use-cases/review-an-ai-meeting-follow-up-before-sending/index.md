## Check the Message That Will Actually Be Sent

In this fictional example, a workshop team needs to send its venue partner a
short follow-up. An AI draft assigns an owner to a proposed change, says the
proof is due "Friday afternoon" and links to an internal planning document.
The internal notes are useful evidence, but the partner does not need them.

Work from approved notes. Make an internal copy for decisions and open
questions, and a separate, shorter message for the external recipient.
Check the same facts in both before sending.

### 1. Separate Commitments from Proposals

Find the wording that establishes each decision. Confirm who accepted the
work, not just who raised the idea or will review it. If an owner or date
was not agreed, leave it open rather than borrowing one from a different
action. Record where the decision appears in the approved notes.

**Try it:** [Check AI meeting actions against decisions](/use-cases/check-ai-meeting-actions-against-decisions/).

### 2. Translate the Deadline Without Changing the Agreement

Confirm the full calendar date, clock time and time zone. For a cross-team
handoff, include the date and time in the recipient's zone as well as the
agreed time zone. Check whether the conversion crosses midnight. If the
notes say only "end of day," ask whose day and what time before converting it.

**Try it:** [Check an AI schedule across time zones](/use-cases/check-an-ai-schedule-across-time-zones/).

### 3. Check What Each Recipient Can See

Read the actual message, links and attachments from the recipient's
perspective. Keep private discussion, raw notes and internal links out of
the version meant for the partner. Confirm that the approved file opens
with the recipient's intended access. Do not widen permissions to get a
test to pass.

**Try it:** [Check an AI redaction before sharing](/use-cases/check-an-ai-redaction-before-sharing/).

## Example: The Same Deadline Falls on Two Days

The approved notes say the proof is due Friday at 16:00 UTC. For the
reviewer at UTC+09:00, that is Saturday at 01:00, not Friday afternoon.
The draft also calls the reviewer the owner, but the notes confirm someone
else will send the proof. **Hold that line until both details are corrected.**
Keep the agreed instant intact; if the local hour is impractical, ask the
owner and reviewer to agree on a new deadline instead of changing it silently.

## Leave a Record for the Next Meeting

Keep the sent version, its source references and any unresolved item
with the approved meeting record. At the next meeting, start by checking
whether each open item gained an owner, a deadline or an explicit decision
to drop it. Keep sensitive details out of the reusable checklist.
