## Goal

Use AI to explore a research question and organize a starting set of notes
without treating an answer, summary, or citation as evidence by itself.

The finished result should make it easy to see the question, the claims that
matter, the original sources you opened, what remains uncertain, and what a
person still needs to verify.

## Define The Question

Write a narrow question before choosing a tool:

- What decision or output will this research support?
- Which date range, location, audience, or field matters?
- What would count as a primary or authoritative source?
- Which claims would change the conclusion if they were wrong?
- What information is sensitive or inappropriate to upload?

A clear scope helps prevent a broad AI response from becoming the research
plan.

## Build A Source-First Workflow

1. Create an initial source map.
   List official documents, primary research, original datasets, direct
   statements, and reputable secondary analysis that may be relevant.

2. Use AI to identify questions, not final answers.
   Ask for terms to investigate, competing explanations, missing context, and
   claims that need evidence.

3. Open every important source.
   Confirm that the page exists, supports the stated claim, matches the date
   and context, and has not been confused with a search snippet or summary.

4. Keep a claim ledger.
   For each important claim, record the source, publication date, relevant
   context, and whether another independent source agrees.

5. Check citations and quotations.
   Verify authors, titles, links, quoted wording, page locations, and whether a
   citation actually supports the sentence attached to it.

6. Record uncertainty.
   Separate verified facts, reasonable interpretations, open questions, and
   unsupported suggestions.

7. Write the synthesis yourself.
   Build the final answer from checked notes and link back to the original
   sources a reader would need.

## Verification Checklist

- Important claims are supported by opened original sources.
- Dates, names, numbers, and quotations match the source.
- Conflicting evidence is visible rather than averaged away.
- The source is current enough for the question.
- AI-generated citations have been checked manually.
- Sensitive documents were handled under the appropriate policy.
- The final wording distinguishes evidence from interpretation.

## Use Extra Care For High-Stakes Research

Academic, legal, medical, financial, public-policy, contractual, and
business-critical research needs additional review. AI can miss exclusions,
methodology limits, jurisdiction, publication updates, retractions, or context.

Follow academic integrity and workplace rules. Do not submit AI-generated work
as your own research without the required review, attribution, and disclosure.
Consult a qualified professional when the decision requires one.
