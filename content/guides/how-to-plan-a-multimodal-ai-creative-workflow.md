## Goal

Plan an AI-assisted creative workflow around the final deliverable, not around
a single impressive generation. Multimodal work may move through text, image,
audio, video, and presentation stages, with different review and rights
questions at each step.

AI output should remain a draft until facts, visual quality, consent, licenses,
brand requirements, disclosure needs, and publishing context have been
checked.

## Define The Deliverable

Write a short creative brief before choosing tools:

- What are you publishing?
- Who is the audience?
- Which formats and dimensions are required?
- Which facts, names, products, or claims must be accurate?
- Which brand assets or style rules apply?
- Who owns the source material?
- Where is human approval required?

A shared brief helps compare tools on workflow fit rather than on isolated
examples.

## Map The Media Stages

1. Outline the message.
   Establish the purpose, factual source material, audience, and call to action
   before generating media.

2. Identify source assets.
   Record which text, images, audio, footage, logos, voices, and likenesses you
   own or are allowed to use.

3. Choose one draft stage at a time.
   Test whether AI is useful for an outline, storyboard, visual concept, rough
   audio, edit, or slide structure without assuming one tool should handle the
   entire project.

4. Keep editable source files.
   Preserve the brief, source material, prompts where appropriate, versions,
   and final human edits so the work can be reviewed and revised.

5. Review each transition.
   Moving from text to image or image to video can introduce new factual,
   visual, timing, rights, and accessibility issues.

6. Approve the final output separately.
   Check the exported file in its real publishing context rather than treating
   a preview as final.

## What To Compare

- **Media fit:** Does the tool support the stage and output type you actually
  need?
- **Review and editing:** Can people inspect and revise the draft before it is
  published?
- **Rights context:** Can you determine the current terms that apply to source
  material, generated output, voices, likenesses, and commercial use?
- **Brand workflow:** Can required logos, colors, terminology, and approval
  steps be handled safely?
- **Export and handoff:** Do current official details support the format and
  downstream workflow you expect?
- **Privacy:** Is the source material appropriate to upload or process?

## Review Facts And Representation

Creative output can invent text, objects, people, product details, citations,
or visual context. Check claims and inspect every frame, slide, caption, and
audio segment that matters.

Do not create deceptive or harmful representations. Obtain appropriate consent
for voices, faces, likenesses, private material, and other third-party content.
Apply additional review to public, political, medical, financial, legal, or
reputation-sensitive communication.

## Check Rights Before Publishing

Review the source rights and current product terms for every stage. Consider
copyright, trademarks, stock assets, music, voice and likeness consent,
attribution, brand use, disclosure, and commercial-use requirements. Tool
availability or a generated result does not establish permission to publish.
