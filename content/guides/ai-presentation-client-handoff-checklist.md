## Start With The Delivery Requirement

Ask the recipient what they need: a view-only file, an editable deck, an online
presentation, or several formats. Confirm where it will be opened and who
will make changes after handoff. Those requirements should drive the trial.

Use the same five-slide brief in each candidate: a problem, one sourced claim,
a comparison, a proposed action, and a source page. Use public or approved
material. This is a trial protocol, not a tested export comparison or a
statement that any listed tool supports a particular format.

## Prepare The Brief Once

```text
Audience and decision the presentation should support:
Five required slide topics:
Approved facts with source links and dates:
Claims the deck must not make:
Approved images, logo, and brand rules:
Required delivery format and receiving application:
Who will edit the deck after delivery:
Final approver:
```

## Test The Handoff, Not Just The Preview

| Stage | What to inspect | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts | Numbers, dates, quotations, comparisons, and chart labels | Original sources and corrected slides |
| Story | Whether each slide supports the requested decision | Notes on omitted or invented claims |
| Brand and media | Permission for logos, images, fonts, and generated assets | Relevant license or approval references |
| Delivery | The file opened in the recipient's actual application | A received-file check, including notes and links |
| Editing | One requested change made after handoff | What remained editable and what needed rebuilding |

If a required export is unavailable in the account you can test, mark delivery
as unverified. Do not promise compatibility based on a web preview or a
marketing description. Confirm current export terms and plan requirements on
the provider's official site before purchasing or committing to a client.

## Run A Recipient Test

Send the harmless sample to a colleague using the target environment. Ask
them to change a heading, replace a chart value, open one source link, and
present the deck. Check cropped text, substituted fonts, missing visuals,
speaker notes, and any animation your brief depends on.

Review contrast and reading order. Text inside images may be difficult to
edit or read with assistive technology. Keep a plain-text outline available
when the audience needs it.

## Keep Rights And Access Visible

Generated output does not establish permission to use a client's branding,
someone's likeness, or a third-party image. Keep provenance notes and resolve
uncertain rights with the responsible owner. Remove confidential prompts,
hidden notes, and unintended sharing permissions before delivery.

Do not paste private strategy documents into an unapproved account to speed
up the trial. Use placeholders until the client's data-use requirements are
settled.

## Record A Practical Decision

Record which candidate delivered the required artifact with an acceptable
amount of human correction. Include the account, trial date, receiving
application, failed checks, and the condition that would trigger another test.
Avoid generalizing a single deck into an overall quality ranking.
