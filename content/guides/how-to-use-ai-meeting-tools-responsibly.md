## Goal

Use AI meeting tools to support a careful notes and follow-up workflow without
losing consent, context, access control, or human responsibility.

A transcript or summary can be useful, but it may be incomplete, incorrect, or
inappropriate to create or share. Recording rules, workplace policies, client
expectations, and legal requirements can vary. Confirm what applies before a
tool joins or records a conversation.

## Decide Whether AI Belongs In The Meeting

Ask these questions first:

- Do all required participants know that AI assistance is being used?
- Is recording or transcription allowed for this meeting and location?
- Does workplace, client, education, or professional policy permit it?
- Will the meeting include personal, confidential, legal, medical, financial,
  employee, customer, or security-sensitive information?
- Who needs the notes, and how long should they remain available?
- Can a simpler manual note-taking process meet the need?

If the purpose, permission, or information boundary is unclear, do not record
or process the meeting.

## Build A Reviewable Workflow

1. Define the note purpose.
   Decide whether you need a transcript, short summary, action list, decisions,
   open questions, or a follow-up draft.

2. Confirm consent and policy.
   Communicate clearly before recording or transcription begins. Follow the
   rules that apply to the participants, organization, and location.

3. Limit access.
   Review connected calendars, meeting accounts, workspaces, participant
   permissions, sharing defaults, and destination folders.

4. Review the source and summary.
   Compare names, decisions, dates, numbers, action owners, and important
   context against the original conversation or approved notes.

5. Correct before sharing.
   Remove unrelated sensitive material, fix errors, and confirm that the
   recipients should have access.

6. Recheck retention and current settings.
   Understand where recordings, transcripts, summaries, and account data are
   stored and how they can be removed. Verify current official product and
   policy details.

## What To Compare

- **Meeting fit:** Does the workflow support the type of meeting and note
  outcome you need?
- **Consent workflow:** Can participants understand when and how AI assistance
  is involved?
- **Review control:** Can a person correct notes and summaries before they are
  shared or relied upon?
- **Access and sharing:** Can you understand who may view recordings, notes,
  transcripts, and follow-up?
- **Data lifecycle:** Can you find current information about storage,
  retention, deletion, and account controls?
- **Current availability:** Do official pages confirm the plan, platform, and
  workflow details you expect?

## Review Before Relying On Notes

AI-generated transcripts and summaries can miss speakers, context, conditions,
negation, numbers, or decisions. They should not become the only record for
legal, medical, financial, contractual, employee, customer, or
business-critical matters.

Confirm decisions and action items with the people responsible. Keep important
records in the approved system rather than assuming an AI-generated summary is
complete or authoritative.

## Handle Sensitive Meetings Carefully

Avoid recording or processing information that should not enter the service.
Use the minimum necessary access and follow security, confidentiality, and
workplace rules. Meetings involving investigations, personnel matters,
credentials, private health information, legal advice, unreleased business
plans, or regulated records need extra scrutiny and may not be suitable.
